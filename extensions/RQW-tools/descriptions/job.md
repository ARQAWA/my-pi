Wait for, check or kill your background jobs. A bash, codemode, read, edit, write, grep or find call still running after 12 seconds returns a job id (job#N) and keeps running. Calls run independently; wait for a job before anything that needs its result.
- wait: if a selected job has already ended it returns its full result at once. Otherwise it returns `reason: "waiting"` at once and your turn ends; do not send another message or poll. One notification message `[job] ...` wakes you later when one of `ids` ends (with any other that ends within a quarter second), at `timeout_ms` with the status of each, or, with `until` (a regular expression), when a job's output matches it. Jobs keep running after a timeout. Estimate how long the job still needs and set `timeout_ms` somewhat longer; 300000 is only the default when omitted. Inside a codemode script or without notifications, a wait still blocks the call.
- status: state, exit code, elapsed time, the last `lines` lines of output (default 20) and the log file of each job in `ids`, or of all your jobs without `ids`. Read the log file with read or grep for more.
- kill: stops a process job and all its child processes. A running file-tool call finishes on its own.
A background job is independent of the user's messages and of Esc: it stops only on `job kill`, a child's interrupt or the end of the session. Kill jobs you no longer need before your final answer, unless the user wants them left running.
